What is not assessed?

Vaccine product and dose, prior vaccination or infection, pregnancy, immune status, underlying conditions, previous reactions, travel and exposure. These factors can change the clinical picture; this prototype does not assess them or determine suitability.

Methods

We use a structured, outcome-by-outcome comparison of disease burden and vaccination evidence, without giving a vaccination recommendation.

  1. Sources: link estimates to clinical studies, evidence reviews, surveillance reports and product information, recording publication dates and the relevant population.
  2. Disease burden: distinguish prevalence, incidence and severity. Retain the region, period and denominator; label global references when regional data are unavailable.
  3. Benefits and harms: preserve the original effect measure and uncertainty interval. Show absolute effects only when a compatible baseline is available; distinguish reported adverse events from attributable vaccine harms.
  4. Evidence quality: assess strengths and weaknesses through study design, bias, precision, follow-up, exclusions, funding, conflicts of interest and applicability. Record missing appraisal items and keep certainty separate from effect size.
  5. Fit to your details: compare the evidence population with the entered age, sex and region. Show subgroup evidence where available; leave unsupported personal estimates unavailable.
  6. The final balance: compare benefits and harms using compatible outcomes, populations and time periods. Display assumptions and gaps; do not convert incompatible figures into a net-benefit score. The prevalence × severity index is a separate exploratory measure, not a personal probability.

The current data coverage and review status are shown alongside the evidence. Missing data do not mean zero risk.
